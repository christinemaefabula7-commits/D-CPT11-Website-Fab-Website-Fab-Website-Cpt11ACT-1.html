PAX SILICA IN THE PHILIPPINES: Opportunity or Concern?

Published: August 13, 2026

Author: Christine Mae Fabula


Pax Silica, What!?

Pax Silica has recently become a topic of discussion in the Philippines because of its possible role in technology, artificial intelligence, semiconductors, and critical minerals. The Philippines joined the U.S.-led initiative in April 2026, opening opportunities for the country to participate more actively in global technology and manufacturing supply chains. A proposed industrial hub in New Clark City is also expected to support industries connected to AI, advanced manufacturing, and semiconductors. A new study found that the end-Permian extinction happened about 252 million years ago and caused the loss of around 90 percent of marine and land species. Researchers found that the mass extinction happened very rapidly, in less than 20,000 years. The study also showed that a large increase in atmospheric carbon dioxide may have caused severe global warming and contributed to the extinction of many species. Widespread wildfires may have also made the environment hotter, drier, and harder for life to survive.

For the Philippines, this could mean new investments, better technology, and more opportunities for Filipino workers and professionals. The Bases Conversion and Development Authority has said that the proposed development could create more than 130,000 high-quality jobs and attract billions of dollars in investments.

However, hindi rin dapat puro benefits lang ang pag-uusapan. Questions about environmental protection, water resources, local communities, and how the country's natural resources will be managed have also been raised. The government has said that the proposed project should follow Philippine laws and include safeguards for the environment and affected communities.

So, is Pax Silica a big opportunity for the Philippines, or should Filipinos be more cautious about it? As this project develops, it is important for us to understand both its possible benefits and its concerns before forming an opinion (NPhilippine News Agency).
